# Licensing

WordPress is open source. When you contribute to it, the terms that apply depend on the project and how you contribute. This page explains what that means and what to check before you contribute.

## What a license does

A license is the set of permissions that comes with a piece of work. It tells everyone what they may do with it, such as use it, change it, and share it. The GPL protects these freedoms for everyone, including the people who build on WordPress.

## The license WordPress uses

WordPress is released under the [GNU General Public License, version 2 or later](https://wordpress.org/about/license/) (GPLv2 or later). It gives everyone [four freedoms](https://wordpress.org/about/#:~:text=anyone%20a%20voice.-,the%20four%20freedoms,-WordPress%20is%20licensed): to run WordPress for any purpose, to study how it works and change it, to share it, and to share the versions they changed.

The WordPress project considers plugins and themes derivative works that inherit the GPL, as the [license page](https://wordpress.org/about/license/) explains. Plugins in the WordPress.org Plugin Directory must be [compatible with the GPL](https://developer.wordpress.org/plugins/wordpress-org/detailed-plugin-guidelines/).

## Your contributions are licensed too

The terms for a contribution depend on the project and how you contribute.

* **On Trac:** when you upload a patch, WordPress Trac shows a notice that by contributing code you grant its use under the GPLv2 (or later), and the upload button reads "Agree and Upload".
* **On GitHub:** [GitHub's Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service#6-contributions-under-repository-license) say that when you add content to a repository with a license notice, you license that content under the same terms, unless a separate agreement, such as a contributor license agreement, says otherwise.

On GitHub, this covers more than code: documentation, images, and other content you add to a repository are licensed under its terms too.

## Some projects use a different license

Not every WordPress project uses the GPL alone. For example:

* Photos for the [WordPress Photo Directory](https://wordpress.org/photos/license/) are submitted under the CC0 license, so anyone can use them for any purpose without permission, attribution, or payment.
* The [Openverse codebase](https://github.com/WordPress/openverse/blob/main/LICENSE) is released under the MIT license.
* Code contributed to [Gutenberg](https://github.com/WordPress/gutenberg/blob/trunk/LICENSE.md) since April 15, 2021 is released under both the GPLv2 (or later) and the Mozilla Public License 2.0.

## Before you contribute

* **Check the license.** Look for a `LICENSE` file in the repository, or the license note in the project's documentation.
* **Only share what you have the right to share.** GitHub's terms ask you to confirm that you have the right to license what you add. Do not copy code, text, or images from somewhere else unless their license allows it.
* **Ask when you are not sure.** The team you are contributing with can help, in their Slack channel or on the issue you are working on.
